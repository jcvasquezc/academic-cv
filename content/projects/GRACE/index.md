GRACE aimed to equip European law enforcement agencies (LEAs) with advanced analytical and investigative capabilities to respond to the spread of online child sexual exploitation material (CSEM). 

Within GRACE, I led the development of multilingual speech recognition and keyword spotting technologies for law enforcement, handling large-scale multilingual datasets and adapting models to unseen forensic domains.

<!--more-->

### Project Overview

The core ambition of GRACE is to deliver an AI-powered, European-wide platform that provides operational value to law enforcement agencies tackling online CSEM. The project focused on four key areas:
1. **Addressing CSEM Referrals:** Providing a scalable route to handle the growing backlog of referrals from online service providers.
2. **Federated Learning Infrastructure:** Deploying secure, privacy-aware federated learning networks across EU Member States to train deep neural models without compromising sensitive data.
3. **Automated Content Analysis:** Delivering multimodal AI-based tools to classify and prioritise CSEM content, allowing LEAs to better safeguard victims.
4. **Trend Tracking:** Facilitating predictive analysis and tracking emerging crime trends in the production and dissemination of CSEM.

### Key Consortium Partners

GRACE brought together law enforcement agencies, universities, and research institutes from 14 Member States. Key partners included:
* **Vicomtech** (Spain) 
* **Europol** (European Union)
* **CENTRIC - Sheffield Hallam University** (United Kingdom)
* **ZITiS** (Germany)
* **Spanish National Police** (Spain)
* **Belgian Federal Police** (Belgium)
* **An Garda Síochána** (Ireland)
